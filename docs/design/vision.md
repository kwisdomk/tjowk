## Purpose

KWAIX.dev exists to present the engineering work, technical trajectory, and operating philosophy of Wisdom Kinoti as a living, evidence-backed technical record.

It is not a portfolio in the traditional sense. A portfolio displays finished work. KWAIX.dev documents ongoing systems, active research, operational decisions, and the reasoning behind them.

The platform should feel like opening an engineer's working notebook — structured, deliberate, and substantive.

---

## Mission

Transform KWAIX.dev from a technically correct portfolio into a premium engineering platform while preserving its evidence-first philosophy.

Every page should answer the question: *"What has this person actually built, and how do they think?"*

---

## Design Philosophy

### Evidence over claims

The site must show work, not describe it. Screenshots, architecture diagrams, terminal output, commit history, deployment records, and technical write-ups are the primary content. Prose exists to connect evidence, not replace it.

### Engineering over marketing

The presentation should feel technical and precise — not promotional. The audience is engineers, hiring managers, and technical reviewers who respect substance. Marketing language erodes trust.

### Operational clarity

The interface should communicate what is active, what is stable, what is planned, and what is archived. Status is information, not decoration.

### Living documentation

The site is not a frozen snapshot. It reflects the current state of ongoing work. Dates, statuses, and operational indicators should always be current and verifiable.

### Technical authenticity

Nothing should be presented that cannot be substantiated. Every project, credential, timeline entry, and metric should trace back to a canonical data source.

Engineers, security professionals, hiring managers, and technical leads who evaluate candidates by examining evidence of actual work rather than reading self-descriptions.

They scan for:
- Depth of technical understanding
- Quality and breadth of implemented systems
- Consistency of engineering practice
- Evidence of learning trajectory
- Operational maturity

### Secondary: Collaborators

Researchers, open-source contributors, hackathon partners, and technical community members who may want to collaborate on cybersecurity, AI, or systems work.

### Tertiary: General visitors

Anyone who arrives via search, social links, or direct referral and needs to quickly understand who Wisdom is and what he builds.

## Experience Goals

When someone visits KWAIX.dev, they should feel:

1. **Confidence** — "This person builds real things."
2. **Clarity** — "I understand what they work on and where they're headed."
3. **Respect** — "The presentation is as thoughtful as the work itself."
4. **Curiosity** — "I want to explore deeper."
5. **Trust** — "Everything here appears verifiable."

They should never feel:

- Overwhelmed by visual noise
- Distracted by meaningless animation
- Suspicious of inflated claims
- Lost in navigation
- Underwhelmed by tiny, unreadable evidence

## Evidence-First Philosophy

This is the foundation of everything.

**Rule**: If a visual element does not help the viewer understand, verify, or evaluate the engineering work, it must justify its existence.

This applies to:
- Colors (do they improve readability and hierarchy?)
- Animations (do they reveal structure or indicate state?)
- Typography (does it guide scanning and improve comprehension?)
- Layout (does it present evidence at a scale where it can be examined?)
- Media (are screenshots large enough to actually read?)
- Components (do cards communicate status and depth, or just fill space?)

The answer to "why does this look this way?" should always be functional, not aesthetic.
